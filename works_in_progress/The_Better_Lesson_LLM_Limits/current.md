## Current formulation

This work investigates why language models can develop strikingly general competence from language while also encountering principled limits. It treats the limit as epistemic rather than merely a shortage of compute: a language-trained system can only draw distinctions that remain recoverable in its representational medium. When the available structure no longer supports a stable distinction, further refinement risks producing noise rather than knowledge.

The positive question is how an architecture could recognize and respond to that limit. Rather than relying only on external engineering restraints, the proposed direction is a system that can distinguish productive learning from arbitrary further differentiation, and can move to other forms of evidence or computation where language has reached its resolving power.
